## Business Rationale

Existing CRMs (e.g., Salesforce/HubSpot) are described as “broken” for day-to-day users because they create heavy admin burden (clicking, manual updates, complex navigation) that pulls time and attention away from selling. This leads to low adoption (reps avoid logging), low trust in CRM data (managers don’t trust it), inaccurate pipelines, and creation of shadow systems (spreadsheets/notes/WhatsApp). The initiative is positioned as a needed shift from “system of record” to “system of action/execution” that reduces cognitive load and tells users the next best action.

## Expected Value

Value is framed as reduced CRM fatigue and cognitive load, higher daily usage/adoption of the CRM, faster execution of sales work (less switching and “after work”), improved data quality/pipeline accuracy via automatic capture/updates, and improved seller experience (calm/clarity/feeling in control). It also claims revenue uplift per rep through better follow-up/next-action guidance, plus improved onboarding speed (minimal or no training).

### Confidence

Medium

**Evidence:** “Main goals are: reduce the general fatigue people have with CRM… get reps to actually use the system daily… increase revenue per rep (if they follow up more, this should happen)… make onboarding basically nonexistent or very fast” / “reduce cognitive load” / “everything logs automatically… pipeline updates itself… no extra steps” / “users… want… to feel in control… not feel overwhelmed… right now CRM = stress + guilt… ideal state = calm, clear.”

### Contradictions

A tension exists between “no dashboards/analytics (at least not upfront)” and the need for trusted manager visibility implied by “Managers don’t trust CRM data,” but no explicit conflict in approach is stated. **Evidence:** “We’re intentionally not doing… analytics dashboards (at least not upfront)” vs “Managers don’t trust CRM data.”

## Assumptions

Assumes that conversational interaction (“one interface (chat)”) and “next action” recommendations will reduce cognitive load and admin burden, leading to higher daily usage and better follow-through (e.g., more follow-ups), which in turn increases revenue per rep. Assumes automation can reliably capture emails/calls and update pipeline stages “in the background” with minimal user input. Assumes minimizing training/onboarding is achievable and desirable (“If someone needs training, we probably failed.”).

## Suggestion

- Make the causal chain explicit and testable (e.g., “next-action guidance + auto-logging → higher activity logging rate → higher follow-up frequency → higher conversion/revenue”), including which links will be validated first.
- Define baseline and measurement method for outcomes (e.g., current NPS, current quarterly subscription revenue, what counts as a “registered user,” attribution approach for “new features result in 20k users”).
- Add measurable adoption/behavior metrics tied to the core problem (e.g., daily active usage, % activities auto-logged, % deals with up-to-date stage, time spent in CRM/admin time reduced).
- State key feasibility/operational assumptions explicitly (e.g., required integrations with email/calendar/telephony, accuracy thresholds for auto-updates, privacy/compliance requirements).
- Clarify audience and initial go-to-market scope with success criteria per segment (sales reps vs founders vs later customer success), to avoid “boil the ocean” drift.
