## Business Rationale

Gentlii exists to address a gap in “agile” product development: faster value delivery does not come from processes alone; it requires significant upfront thinking, planning, and product context-building, but most product professionals lack the time and environment to do this. The initiative also responds to industry trends that overemphasize spec-driven work and AI output generation rather than improving product quality and outcomes.

### Confidence
High

**Evidence:** “the agile promise of faster value delivery does not come from the processes like scrum”; “it requires much more thinking and planning”; “most people do not have the time nor the head space”; “the focus is on spec-driven development… start building too early without enough product context”; “Output over outcome while is must be the other way around.”
  
**Contradictions:** No contradictory content found.

## Expected Value

Gentlii is expected to reduce cognitive load for product professionals by creating and maintaining a concise, trustworthy “product description” (strategy, product vision, business case, product charter) from available data. It also aims to improve product quality and alignment by guarding consistency across product artifacts and validating feature requests against the product description, enabling quicker stakeholder clarity and reducing waste from building misaligned features.

### Confidence
High

**Evidence:** “This product is about reducing cognitive load of product professionals”; “provide help in gathering the needed information for solid product development… opinionated product definition”; “making sure they can rely on the quality of this product definition without any required action”; “guard alignment between all mentioned output documents”; “validate feature requests against the product description”; “give clarity and quick feedback.”
  
**Contradictions:** No contradictory content found.

## Assumptions

- Product professionals will provide (upload) sufficient and relevant existing input materials (“all types of files”) to extract accurate meaning and generate the core product documents.
- Users will accept an “opinionated” product definition and trust the output quality “without any required action.”
- Integrating into existing tools/workflows (and minimizing UI/account overhead) is feasible and will reduce cognitive load rather than adding friction.
- Feature validation outcomes (e.g., “Not good”) can be meaningfully recorded and used as a proxy for avoided waste.

### Confidence
Medium

**Evidence:** “A user uploads all types of files they can find that state something about the product”; “created by extracting the meaning from all available data”; “opinionated product definition”; “rely on the quality… without any required action”; “should integrate within existing tools and workflows”; “we can register how many feature requests are marked Not good.”

## Measurable Business Outcomes

Stated success measures include:
- Reduce time to create a proper product description/context by 75%.
- Improve product description quality “to a state where it is confidently right on all facets” (implied to be “backed up by data,” but no explicit metric is defined).
- Reduce the number of features built that are not the right solution by 50% (tracked via feature requests marked “Not good”).
- Improve the product with less code, saving 25% on “operations, bugs and other expensive stuff.”
- Provide stakeholders quicker verification/feedback on feature request feasibility (directionally stated; no target time metric).

## Suggestion

- Define how “quality of the product description” will be measured (e.g., rubric dimensions, stakeholder agreement rate, rework reduction, defect/decision reversal rate).
- Specify baseline and measurement method for “75% time reduction” (what activities are included; from/to; sample size; time period).
- Clarify the causal link and measurement for “25% ops/bugs savings” (which cost buckets, how attributed to “less code,” and over what timeframe).
- Make key assumptions explicit and testable (e.g., minimum viable input data set quality/quantity; required integrations; acceptable confidence thresholds for auto-generated artifacts).
- Add outcome metrics for “quick feedback” (e.g., median time-to-answer for feature feasibility; stakeholder satisfaction/NPS for decision clarity).
