## Business Rationale

Current CRMs (e.g., Salesforce) are described as “broken” for frontline users because they create high administrative overhead (clicking, updating fields, dashboards), fragmented workflows, and stress/guilt from being behind. The proposed product rationale is to shift CRM from a system users maintain into a conversation-first assistant that tells users what to do next, automates capture/updates, and reduces decision-making and context switching—so reps/founders can focus on selling and execution rather than administration.

**Confidence:** High

**Evidence:** “CRM as it exists today (like Salesforce etc.) is kind of broken… It’s too much clicking around, updating fields, checking dashboards… feels like admin work instead of helping you sell.” / “What we want instead is something that just… tells you what to do next.” / “today they usually check like 3–5 things (CRM, email, calendar, maybe Slack) → already fragmented” / “right now CRM = stress + guilt (because it’s always behind)”

**Contradictions:** No contradictory content found.

## Expected Value

Expected value centers on improved daily adoption, reduced CRM fatigue/admin burden, faster onboarding, and improved sales effectiveness via prioritized “next best actions” and automation (auto logging, background pipeline updates, quick execution). There is an implied revenue uplift through more consistent follow-up and fewer missed actions.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Evidence:** “Main goals are: reduce the general fatigue people have with CRM… get reps to actually use the system daily… increase revenue per rep (if they follow up more, this should happen)… make onboarding basically nonexistent or very fast” / “automatic logging of emails / calls… pipeline updates happening in the background… quick actions”

## Assumptions

The material assumes that (1) users primarily want clear next actions rather than dashboards/analytics, (2) reducing manual entry and navigation will increase daily usage, (3) automation can reliably capture emails/calls and keep pipeline stages updated “in the background,” (4) increased follow-up driven by the system will increase revenue per rep, and (5) minimizing training/onboarding is feasible if the product is designed correctly. It also assumes conversation-first UI can replace most traditional CRM screens.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Evidence:** “people don’t need more charts, they need to know what to do” / “if user has to fill fields → something went wrong” / “everything updates in background, invisible” / “increase revenue per rep (if they follow up more, this should happen)” / “If someone needs training, we probably failed.” / “conversation first, always… no screens if we can avoid it”

## Suggestion

Add concrete, testable metrics and targets to turn stated goals into a usable business case—for example: define baseline vs target “daily usage” (e.g., % of reps active daily), quantify “CRM fatigue” via a survey score or time-in-admin per day, specify onboarding time target (e.g., time-to-first-value), and set a revenue/performance proxy tied to follow-up (e.g., follow-ups sent per rep, response rate, pipeline progression, revenue per rep) with an evaluation timeframe; also explicitly state key feasibility assumptions (email/call capture coverage, accuracy of auto stage updates, compliance constraints) and how they will be validated in pilots.
