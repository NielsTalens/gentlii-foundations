## Business Rationale

Existing CRMs (e.g., Salesforce/HubSpot) are described as “broken” for frontline users because they impose heavy manual admin, complex navigation, fragmented workflows, low adoption, and unreliable data—leading to less time selling and missed follow-ups. The initiative exists to redefine CRM from a “system of record” into a “system of action/execution” that reduces cognitive load and tells users the next best action.

High

**Evidence:** “CRM as it exists today (like Salesforce etc.) is kind of broken… It’s too much clicking around… feels like admin work instead of helping you sell.” / “Teams using systems like Salesforce or HubSpot often experience: Heavy manual data entry… Time spent updating instead of selling… Complex navigation” / “Reps avoid logging activities… Managers don’t trust CRM data… Pipeline is inaccurate… Shadow systems… emerge” / “We want CRM as a system of execution.”

## Expected Value

Time and cognitive-load reduction for reps (less admin, less switching, fewer decisions), increased daily CRM usage/adoption, improved pipeline accuracy/trust via automatic logging and background updates, faster onboarding (minimal/no training), and revenue lift by enabling more consistent follow-up and execution.

### Confidence

High

**Evidence:** “reduce the general fatigue people have with CRM” / “get reps to actually use the system daily” / “increase revenue per rep (if they follow up more, this should happen)” / “make onboarding basically nonexistent or very fast” / “We need to to reduce cognitive load, not just time” / “everything logs automatically… pipeline updates itself… basically zero data entry.”

## Assumptions

Value depends on users preferring a conversation-first interface over dashboards/screens; that the system can accurately infer/prioritize “next best action” and update pipeline/stages “in the background” without user input; that automatic capture/logging of calls/emails is feasible and reliable; and that reduced friction leads to higher follow-up rates, daily usage, and increased revenue per rep.

## Suggestion

- Define baseline and targets for adoption/usage (e.g., % weekly active reps, activities logged per rep, follow-up SLA adherence) to support “use the system daily.”
- Specify measurable onboarding goals (e.g., time-to-first-value, time-to-configure, training hours = 0) to substantiate “onboarding… very fast.”
- Make key assumptions testable (e.g., accuracy of next-best-action recommendations, % correct auto-logged activities, % correct pipeline stage updates).
- Link revenue/user-growth targets to a causal measurement plan (how “next action” and automation drive EUR 50k/q and 20k users; leading indicators and attribution).
- Clarify scope constraints vs future plans (e.g., “no dashboards” vs potential manager needs) and define if/when minimal reporting is introduced without violating principles.
