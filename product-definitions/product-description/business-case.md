## Business Rationale

Current CRMs are described as “broken” for frontline users because they create heavy admin overhead (clicking, updating fields, dashboards) and fragmented workflows across multiple tools (CRM, email, calendar, Slack). The proposed product reframes CRM as a conversation-first system that prioritizes telling users the next best action and automating capture/updates, aiming to reduce stress/fatigue and make the system effectively “invisible.”

**Confidence:** High

**Evidence:** “CRM as it exists today (like Salesforce etc.) is kind of broken… It’s too much clicking around, updating fields, checking dashboards… feels like admin work instead of helping you sell.”; “today they usually check like 3–5 things (CRM, email, calendar, maybe Slack) → already fragmented”; “people don’t want to use a system they want the system to tell them what to do”; “Feels like CRM should become kind of invisible over time.”

**Contradictions:** No contradictory content found.

## Expected Value

The expected value is improved CRM adoption and productivity by eliminating manual data entry and reducing cognitive load: users get prioritized next actions, can execute follow-ups quickly, and have calls/emails and pipeline changes logged automatically. Business-side expectations include reduced CRM fatigue, daily usage, increased revenue per rep through better follow-up, and faster onboarding with minimal training.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Evidence:** “Main goals are: reduce the general fatigue people have with CRM… get reps to actually use the system daily… increase revenue per rep (if they follow up more, this should happen)… make onboarding basically nonexistent or very fast”; “log stuff automatically (calls, emails etc.)”; “keep the pipeline updated without you touching it”; “action executes immediately… everything logs automatically in background.”

**Contradictions:** No contradictory content found.

## Assumptions

The business case relies on assumptions that (1) reducing admin and decision effort will drive daily usage, (2) increased follow-up activity will increase revenue per rep, and (3) a conversation-first/no-screens approach can replace traditional CRM navigation without requiring training. It also assumes automatic capture/logging and background pipeline updates are feasible and “table stakes.”

**Confidence:** Medium

**Evidence:** “increase revenue per rep (if they follow up more, this should happen)”; “If someone needs training, we probably failed.”; “automatic logging of emails / calls (this is table stakes imo)”; “conversation first, always… no screens if we can avoid it”; “everything updates in background, invisible.”

**Contradictions:** Potential tension between “conversation first, always / no screens” and acknowledging uncertainty about feasibility (“Not 100% sure how far we can push that”).

**Evidence:** “conversation first, always… no screens if we can avoid it” vs. “Not 100% sure how far we can push that but that’s the direction.”
