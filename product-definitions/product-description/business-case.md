## Business Rationale

Gentlii exists to address a gap in agile/product development where “faster value delivery” does not come from processes alone, but from better upfront thinking, planning, and product context—something many product professionals lack time and environment for. It also positions itself against industry trends of spec-driven development and “output over outcome,” arguing teams start building too early without enough context. Additionally, it is framed as an extension/scaling of existing product thinking consultancy that is currently too dependent on consultants.

## Expected Value

Improve product quality by generating and maintaining aligned product artifacts (strategy, vision, charter, business case), validating feature requests against the product description, and shifting focus from output to outcomes.

## Assumptions

- Users can provide “all types of files” with sufficient signal about the product/ideas, and Gentlii can extract meaning from “all available data” to generate correct core product artifacts.
- Users will trust the generated product definition “without any required action,” implying the system can achieve and maintain high quality autonomously.
- Integration into existing tools/workflows (and “invisible” operation) is feasible without becoming “an additional app with an UI that needs an account.”
- Quality of product definition can be assessed and “proved (backed up by data).”

- Save 25% on operations/bugs/“expensive stuff” by “better features with less code.”

- Improve product description quality to a level that is “confidently right on all facets,” with proof “backed up by data” (measurement method not specified).

## Suggestion

- Define how “quality of the product description” will be measured (rubric, acceptance criteria, baseline, and how “confidently right on all facets” is validated).
- Specify the measurement approach for “25% op operations, bugs” (which cost buckets, timeframe, and attribution to Gentlii vs other factors).
- Make key assumptions explicit and testable (e.g., minimum viable input data needed; what “no required action” means operationally; limits/failure modes).
- Clarify the target users, usage context, and the “integration within existing tools” scope (which tools first, what “invisible” interaction looks like).
- Add a causal chain narrative connecting inputs → product description → alignment/validation → fewer wrong features → time/cost savings.
