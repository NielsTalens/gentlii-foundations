## Business Rationale

Gentlii exists to address a gap between the “agile promise” of faster value delivery and the reality that achieving it requires significant upfront product thinking, planning, and context-building—work that product professionals often lack time and “head space” to do. The initiative is also positioned as a response to industry trends (spec-driven development and output-over-outcome) that lead teams to build too early without sufficient product context, reducing product quality.

Gentlii is also framed as a scalable/extendable complement to existing product thinking consultancy, reducing reliance on a consultant to “pull” the process.

## Expected Value

Gentlii is expected to reduce cognitive load for product professionals by integrating into existing workflows, producing a concise and reliable “product definition/description” from available data, and continuously guarding alignment between strategy/vision/charter and downstream feature requests. Value is also expected through faster creation of product context, better decision-making and clarity for stakeholders, and avoiding building misaligned features—saving time and money and improving product quality with less code.

## Assumptions

- Product professionals have sufficient existing “available data” (files/artifacts) that can be uploaded and meaningfully synthesized into strategy, vision, business case, and charter documents.  
- The generated product description can be made “confidently right on all facets” without requiring user action, and stakeholders will trust and use it for verification/decision-making.  
- Integrating into existing tools/workflows (and being “invisible”) is feasible and will reduce cognitive load rather than add process burden.  
- Validating feature requests against the product description will meaningfully reduce misbuilt features and associated costs.

## Suggestion

- Define how “quality of the product description” will be measured (dimensions, scoring method, baseline, and target) to make “confidently right on all facets” testable.  
- Make assumptions explicit and testable (e.g., minimum viable input data required; what “no required action” means operationally; trust/adoption conditions).  
- Clarify the causal chain from alignment-guarding/feature validation to the 50% reduction in wrong features (e.g., decision points, enforcement mechanism, who acts on “Not good”).  
- Specify measurement approach and baselines for “25% ops/bugs savings” (what costs are included, time horizon, attribution method).  
- Add a clear economic framing (e.g., primary cost drivers today; where time/money is lost; how savings translate to ROI) to strengthen the business case narrative.
