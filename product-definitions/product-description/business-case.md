## Business Rationale
Gentlii exists to address a gap between agile processes and actually delivering value faster: faster value delivery requires significant thinking, planning, and product context-building, but product professionals often lack the time/headspace and supportive environment to do that work. The initiative is also positioned against trends that over-focus on specs and AI-generated output rather than product quality and outcomes. Additionally, it is framed as a scalable extension of existing “product thinking consultancy,” reducing dependence on a consultant to drive the process.

## Expected Value
Gentlii is expected to reduce cognitive load for product professionals and improve the quality and reliability of core product-definition artifacts (strategy, vision, charter, measurable outcomes). It aims to improve alignment by “guarding” consistency across documents and validating feature requests against the product description, leading to fewer wrong features built and better product decisions with less effort. It also aims to integrate into existing tools/workflows and be “invisible,” avoiding the overhead of yet another standalone UI/app.

**Confidence:** High  
**Evidence:** “This product is about reducing cognitive load of product professionals.”; “providing help in gathering the needed information for solid product development…”; “making sure they can rely on the quality of this product definition…”; “providing a way for them to check whether any idea aligns…”; “Wherever possible the product should integrate within existing tools and workflows.”; “The product will guard alignment…”; “The product will validate feature requests against the product description.”

## Assumptions
- High-quality product context/definitions upstream lead to better downstream decisions (feature selection, alignment, reduced waste) and faster value delivery.  
- Sufficient “all available data” exists and can be uploaded/accessed so agents can extract meaning to produce accurate strategy/vision/charter/business-case documents.  
- Users will trust and adopt an “opinionated product definition” with “concise” descriptions and minimal/no UI, and will use feature-validation against it.  
- Detecting changes in input files (CRUD) and running a “Product Guard” report will be feasible within users’ workflows/tooling.

## Measurable Business Outcomes
- Reduce time to create a proper product description/context by 75%.  
- Reduce the amount of features built that are not the right solution by 50% (tracked via how many feature requests are marked “Not good”).  
- Save 25% on “operations, bugs and other expensive stuff” by improving the product with less code / better features with less code.  
- Improve product-description quality “to a state where it is confidently right on all facets” (claimed to be backed by data, but measurement method not defined).  
- Provide faster stakeholder feedback/clarity on feature request feasibility (speed not quantified).

## Suggestion
- Define how “quality of the product description” will be measured (rubric, scoring, defect rate, stakeholder agreement rate, rework rate) and set target thresholds.  
- Quantify “quick feedback” (e.g., median time-to-answer for feature feasibility) and specify the baseline.  
- Make key assumptions explicit and testable (e.g., minimum input data types/coverage required; expected adoption/trust conditions; integration prerequisites).  
- Clarify the causal chain from “better product definition” to “25% ops/bugs savings” (what categories, how attributed, over what time period).  
- Add a clear target segment and context (team size, product maturity, tool stack) to reduce ambiguity about where the outcomes apply.
