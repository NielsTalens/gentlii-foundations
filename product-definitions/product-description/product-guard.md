# Strategy ↔ Business Case

### Alignment score
5/5

### Confidence
High

## Alignment themes
Both documents frame the core problem and solution the same way: traditional CRMs create admin overhead and cognitive load; the product should shift CRM from “system of record” to “system of action/execution,” using a conversational interface that tells users the “next best action,” with automation-by-default to remove manual logging and keep the pipeline updated.  
**Evidence:** Strategy mission: “Replace complex… CRM workflows with a conversational ‘system of action/execution’ that tells users exactly what to do next, reducing cognitive load.” / Business rationale: “Shift from system-of-record… We want CRM as a system of execution.” + “reduce cognitive load.”

## Detected contradictions
Both sources contain the same tension: “avoid dashboards/reporting” vs user requests for “more insights” and “create their own dashboards.”  
**Evidence:** Strategy pillars: “Actions over insights… avoid anything that looks like reporting.” vs customer event: “Users want to have more insights… create their own dashboards.” / Business case: “they don’t want dashboards” vs “Users want… dashboards.”

## Missing links
The business case includes numeric outcomes, but the causal chain and leading/adoption metrics are not fully specified or consistently defined across the pair (e.g., “daily usage,” “onboarding basically nonexistent” appear without measurement detail).  
**Evidence:** Strategy success metrics include “get reps to actually use the system daily” and “make onboarding basically nonexistent,” but without targets/definitions; business case similarly notes adoption/onboarding goals “without specific metrics/targets.”

## Minimal change to improve coherence
Add one explicit, shared stance on the dashboards/insights tension (e.g., “no dashboards upfront; only ‘why this?’ explanations and action-converting signals”) and add 2–3 shared leading metrics definitions (e.g., DAU/WAU, % activities auto-logged, time-to-first-value) tied to the numeric business outcomes.  
**Evidence:** Charter/business/strategy repeatedly assert “avoid… dashboards” and also note “Users want… dashboards”; both documents include adoption/onboarding goals without defined measurement.


# Business Case ↔ Product Vision

### Alignment score
5/5

### Confidence
High

## Alignment themes
The vision’s product concept directly implements the business case rationale and expected value: conversation-first UX, “next best action,” automation for logging and background pipeline updates, and the goal of higher adoption/lower fatigue with minimal training—positioned as moving from “record” to “execution.”  
**Evidence:** Product vision: “Replace complex CRM software with a conversational system that tells you exactly what to do next… system of action/execution… invisible… reduces cognitive load.” / Business case: “CRM… broken… admin work… shift… system of execution” + expected value: “higher daily adoption… onboarding basically nonexistent… automatic logging… pipeline updates.”

## Detected contradictions
Shared contradiction/tension appears again: de-emphasizing reporting/dashboards vs user feedback asking for insights and custom dashboards.  
**Evidence:** Vision differentiators: “Actions > insights… avoid anything that looks like reporting” vs “Users want… dashboards.” / Business case contradictions cite the same quotes.

## Missing links
The vision lists business goals and the business case lists measurable outcomes, but the explicit mapping between product features → leading indicators → the numeric outcomes (EUR 50k/quarter, 20k users, NPS 7→9) is not laid out as a connected chain across the two artifacts.  
**Evidence:** Business case: “Increase of revenu of EUR 50.000,- per quarter…” etc.; vision: goals like “Increase daily adoption/usage” and “Increase revenue per rep” without a defined linkage model.

## Minimal change to improve coherence
Add a short “logic model” section to either document that explicitly ties vision features (next-best-action, auto-logging, background updates) to measurable leading indicators and then to the business outcomes.  
**Evidence:** Business case includes outcomes; vision includes features/goals; neither provides an explicit end-to-end chain beyond implied statements like “increase revenue per rep (if they follow up more…).”


# Product Vision ↔ Product Charter

### Alignment score
5/5

### Confidence
High

## Alignment themes
The charter operationalizes the vision with concrete principles, boundaries, and behavioral rules: conversation-first, actions-over-insights, automation-by-default/zero data entry, reduced choices/cognitive load, and “invisible CRM,” plus rules like proactive suggestions and short “why” explanations rather than reporting.  
**Evidence:** Vision: “conversational system… tells you exactly what to do next… invisible… reduces cognitive load.” / Charter principles: “Conversation-first… Actions over insights… Automation by default… Reduce cognitive load… CRM should become ‘invisible’ over time.” / Charter behavioral rules: “system suggests actions before you ask” + “short explanation (not a report).”

## Detected contradictions
Both acknowledge (directly or via cited research notes) the same tension: user demand for dashboards/insights conflicts with the charter’s explicit boundaries against reporting/BI and dashboards (at least upfront).  
**Evidence:** Charter boundaries: “Not a reporting/BI tool” + “analytics dashboards (at least not upfront)” vs customer notes: “Users want… dashboards.” / Vision notes same tension: “avoid anything that looks like reporting” vs “Users want… dashboards.”

## Missing links
The vision introduces target groups and broad feature ideas, while the charter defines constraints and integrity checks; what’s missing is an explicit policy that reconciles “why/insight” needs with “no dashboards,” beyond “short explanation (not a report).”  
**Evidence:** Charter: “If user asks ‘why this?’ provide a short explanation (not a report)” but also “Not a reporting/BI tool” and “analytics dashboards (at least not upfront)”—no further stated handling of “more insights/custom dashboards” requests.

## Minimal change to improve coherence
Add one explicit “insights policy” clause to the charter (and reference it in the vision differentiators): allow only action-converting signals and “why” explanations; explicitly disallow custom dashboards (or define a phased exception) to resolve the recurring tension.  
**Evidence:** Charter suggestion already points to this: “Define an explicit policy for handling requests for ‘insights/dashboards’…” and the recurring contradiction quotes appear in both vision and charter.
