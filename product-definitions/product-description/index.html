Product Strategy

Gentlii focuses on digital product development by reducing the cognitive load of product professionals through an “opinionated product definition” / “product description” that is automatically created from existing product inputs, kept aligned, and used to validate new ideas and feature requests. The product should be largely “invisible” by integrating into existing tools and workflows (not “another app” with accounts/UI), and should prioritize outcomes over output (countering spec-driven and output-focused trends).

Confidence: High

Evidence: “Gentlii focusses on digital product development.”; “reducing cognitive load of product professionals. Therefore it should not be an additional app with an UI that needs an account”; “Wherever possible the product should integrate within existing tools and workflows.”; “providing help in gathering the needed information… capture this in a opinionated product definition.”; “The product will create strategic, product vision, product charter documents… extracting the meaning from all available data.”; “The product will validate feature requests against the product description.”; “Defining outcomes is very important. More important than output.”

Contradictions:

No contradictory content found.

Value Proposition

Automate creation of a concise, high-quality product definition/context from available data; ensure stakeholders can trust it “without any required action”; continuously guard alignment across strategy/vision/charter documents; and quickly validate whether feature requests align—saving time/money and reducing wasted development.

Confidence: High

Business Rationale

Gentlii is positioned as a response to the gap between “agile” process adoption and actually delivering value faster: teams need more thinking, planning, and context-building than they have time/headspace for, and industry trends over-focus on specs and AI-generated output rather than outcomes/quality. The product also connects to an existing “product thinking consultancy” model, aiming to reduce dependency on consultants by embedding the process into a product.

Confidence: High

Evidence: “the agile promise of faster value delivery does not come from the processes like scrum”; “it requires much more thinking and planning”; “most people do not have the time nor the head space”; “focus is on spec-driven development… start building too early without enough product context”; “Output over outcome”; “Gentlii could be a extension of the product thinking consultancy work.”

Contradictions:

No contradictory content found.

Expected Value

Expected value centers on reducing cognitive load and integrating into existing workflows (not adding another UI-heavy app), producing concise but trusted product definitions, improving alignment, and reducing wasted build work and associated costs (time/money, operations, bugs). It also aims to provide faster clarity/feedback to stakeholders on feature requests.

Confidence: High

Evidence: “reducing cognitive load of product professionals… should not be an additional app with an UI”; “integrate within existing tools and workflows”; “need a very concise product definition”; “rely on the quality of this product definition”; “provide a way for them to check whether any idea aligns with the product definition”; “give clarity and quick feedback”; “time and money saved.”

Contradictions:

No contradictory content found.

Assumptions

Assumes product professionals lack time/headspace for necessary product thinking due to their environment; assumes a “product description/definition” can be created by extracting meaning from “all available data” and can be trusted “without any required action”; assumes feature requests can be validated against the product description and that “Not good” markings can be used to register/measure avoided waste; assumes the product should be “invisible” and integrated into existing tools rather than a standalone app.

Confidence: Medium

Evidence: “most people do not have the time nor the head space”; “The environment doesn't allow it”; “created by extracting the meaning from all available data”; “rely on the quality… without any required action”; “validate feature requests against the product description”; “we can register how many feature requests are marked Not good”; “Wherever possible… integrate within existing tools”; “Wherever we can make the product invisible we will do that.”

Measurable Business Outcomes

The text specifies target improvements including time reduction to create product context/description, reduction in wrong features built, and operational savings from “less code / better features.” It also mentions proving quality improvement “backed up by data,” though without a numeric target or timeframe.

Confidence: High

Evidence: “Reduce the time to create a proper product description/context by 75%.”; “reduce the amount of features that will be build that are not the right solution by 50%”; “save 25% op operations, bugs and other expensive stuff”; “prove (bakced up by data) that the quality… will improve to a state where it is confidently right on all facets.”

Contradictions:

No contradictory content found.

Suggestion

  • Add timeframes for each target (e.g., “within 3 months of rollout”) to make outcomes actionable.
  • Define how “quality… confidently right on all facets” will be measured (rubric, audit process, stakeholder score, error rate).
  • Specify baseline assumptions behind the 75%/50%/25% improvements (current cycle time, current % misaligned features, current ops/bug costs).
  • Clarify what “integrate within existing tools” means (which tools first) and how “invisible” delivery will work in practice.
  • State a clear business model hypothesis (e.g., consultancy extension packaging, pricing, target customer segment) to strengthen the business case.
