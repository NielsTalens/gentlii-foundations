## Company Strategy

Build a differentiated CRM company by positioning against legacy CRMs (e.g., Salesforce/HubSpot) as “broken” for day-to-day sellers, and focusing on a narrow initial segment (sales reps and founders) rather than broad, enterprise-style feature breadth (“not trying to boil the ocean”). The strategy emphasizes adoption through reduced friction (minimal training, daily usage), and differentiation via conversation-first interaction, automation, and action-orientation (not analytics/reporting).

## Product Strategy

Create a conversation-first CRM (chat as primary UI; voice possibly later) that minimizes or removes traditional screens/tabs/dashboards and instead provides prioritized “next best actions,” executes actions quickly, and automates data capture and pipeline updates in the background (“invisible” CRM). Product principles: actions over insights, automation by default, reduce choices, and avoid drifting into reporting/BI or heavy configurability.

**Confidence:** High  
**Evidence:** “It’s basically a CRM but conversation-first. Probably chat based, maybe voice later. No real ‘screens’”; “tell you your next best action”; “log stuff automatically… keep the pipeline updated without you touching it”; “conversation first, always… no screens if we can avoid it”; “actions > insights”; “automation by default”; “system suggests actions before you ask (ideally)”; “not a reporting tool… not BI… not something you customize endlessly.”  
**Contradictions:** No contradictory content found.

## Value Proposition

Reduce CRM “admin work” and fragmentation by telling users exactly what to do next (prioritized), enabling immediate execution (send/call/schedule), and keeping records/pipeline updated automatically—resulting in a calmer, more controlled feeling for users and better follow-through.

**Confidence:** High  
**Evidence:** “What we want instead is something that just… tells you what to do next.”; “Not another tool you manage, more like something that manages you”; “they want to know what to do next, clearly… and they want everything to be fast”; “everything logs automatically… pipeline updates itself… no extra steps, no ‘after work’”; “ideal state = calm, clear, just doing the next thing.”

## Strategic Goals

- Reduce CRM fatigue and perceived admin burden.  
- Drive daily usage/adoption of the system.  
- Increase revenue per rep (via improved follow-up).  
- Make onboarding/training minimal or “nonexistent.”  
- Achieve “basically zero data entry” through automation.  
- Avoid becoming a reporting/BI tool or a traditional CRM experience.

## Suggestion

Add sharper strategic boundaries and success metrics to make the strategy more actionable—for example: define the initial wedge persona (e.g., “founder-led sales teams 1–10 reps” vs. generic “sales reps”), specify required integrations (email/calendar/calling) for “automatic logging,” and set concrete targets like “<10 minutes onboarding,” “>70% daily active usage,” “0 manual stage updates for X% of deals,” and “measurable lift in follow-up rate/revenue per rep,” alongside explicit non-goals (e.g., “no custom objects/workflows v1,” “no dashboards until v2”).
