## Product Strategy

Build a conversation-first CRM (chat-based initially, potentially voice later) that replaces traditional CRM navigation (screens, tabs, dashboards) with a “what should I do now?” interaction model. The system should proactively surface prioritized next-best actions, enable immediate execution (send/call/schedule), and automate background logging and pipeline updates to minimize or eliminate manual data entry. Product principles emphasize “actions > insights,” automation by default, reduced choices, and avoiding drift into a traditional CRM or reporting/BI tool.

## Value Proposition

Reduce CRM admin burden and fragmentation by giving salespeople a fast, calm, decisive assistant that tells them the next action, executes it quickly, and keeps CRM data updated invisibly. The promise is less stress/guilt and more follow-through (and therefore improved revenue outcomes) without dashboards, reporting, or manual updates.

## Strategic Goals

- Reduce “general fatigue” people have with CRM.
- Get reps to use the system daily.
- Increase revenue per rep (via better follow-up).
- Make onboarding “nonexistent or very fast” (training implies failure).

**Confidence:** High

**Evidence:** “Main goals are: reduce the general fatigue… get reps to actually use the system daily… increase revenue per rep… make onboarding basically nonexistent or very fast. If someone needs training, we probably failed.”

## Long-term Direction

Move toward a CRM that becomes “invisible” over time—always updating in the background, proactively suggesting actions, and minimizing user choices and interface surface area (avoiding reversion into screens/dashboards). There is an explicit direction to push further toward that invisibility, with some acknowledged uncertainty about limits.

## Suggestion

- Define clear target segment sequencing and entry wedge (e.g., reps vs founders first) with explicit “initial ICP” criteria.
- Add measurable success metrics/targets (e.g., daily active use, time saved per rep, follow-up rate, revenue-per-rep uplift).
- Clarify MVP scope vs “later” (e.g., what automation/logging integrations are required at launch; when voice is considered).
- State explicit strategic differentiators vs incumbents in one place (beyond “not dashboards”), including how recommendations are generated and kept trustworthy.
- Document key risks/constraints that could force “drift” (e.g., auditability, compliance, edge cases requiring screens) and how you’ll handle them without breaking principles.
